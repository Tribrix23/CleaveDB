1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Most modern database development suffers from "multi-model fatigue." Systems
attempt to bundle Document, Graph, Full-Text, and AI/Vector embeddings into a
single monolithic engine (e.g., ArangoDB, SurrealDB, CosmosDB). In computer 
science systems research, such combinations are viewed as engineering convenience,
not scientific breakthroughs. Vector search has rapidly become a commoditized
library dependency (HNSW / Flat SIMD) rather than a foundational paradigm.

To stand alongside the historical breakthroughs in database research:
  - System R / SQL (1970s): Codd's Relational Algebra (declarative sets, closed-world FKs)
  - Neo4j / Graph DBs (2000s): Index-Free Adjacency (pointer-chasing across localized graphs)
  - MonetDB / ClickHouse (2010s): Vectorized Columnar Execution & SIMD Run-Length Scans
  - Materialize / Flink (2020s): Differential Dataflow & Incremental View Maintenance

CleaveDB must establish its OWN foundational database paradigm. That primitive
must address a pervasive, unsolved problem in distributed data systems:

  THE DISTRIBUTED REFERENTIAL INTEGRITY PARADOX:
  How can a distributed system guarantee absolute structural and referential
  integrity when writes, events, and partitions arrive asynchronously and out of order,
  WITHOUT paying the throughput-destroying penalty of Two-Phase Commit (2PC)
  or global distributed locking?


2. THE 55-YEAR DISTRIBUTED REFERENTIAL INTEGRITY PARADOX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In 1970, Edgar Codd formulated the Closed-World Relational Model: relations assume
synchronous existence. A child row cannot be inserted unless its referenced parent
row already exists at the exact instant of transaction commitment.

In modern computing, data is fundamentally asynchronous:
  - Microservices emit decoupled event streams across Kafka, RabbitMQ, or NATS.
  - Multi-region database clusters ingest data across WAN latencies.
  - Edge gateways, mobile devices, and IoT nodes sync intermittently while offline.

In all of these architectures: DATA WRITES INEVITABLY ARRIVE OUT OF ORDER.

This forces a catastrophic trade-off across existing database engines:

  A) Traditional Relational (SQL - Postgres, Oracle, MySQL):
     Foreign Key constraints enforce strict synchronous validation. If an order
     arrives before customer data has synced across shards or services, the write
     fails with a constraint violation ("foreign key constraint fails").
     Attempting to preserve cross-shard foreign keys requires distributed Two-Phase
     Locking (2PL) and Two-Phase Commit (2PC). This causes lock thrashing, latency
     spikes, and distributed deadlocks.
     RESULT: Virtually every high-scale tech company (Uber, Netflix, Amazon)
     explicitly disables Foreign Keys in distributed production systems.

  B) Distributed NoSQL / Document / Key-Value (MongoDB, Cassandra, DynamoDB):
     By discarding foreign keys, writes become fast, local, and non-blocking.
     However, this creates "Referential Decay": databases accumulate orphaned
     child records, dangling foreign identifiers, and silent corruption.
     Engineers are forced to write fragile, ad-hoc background "janitor scripts"
     to continuously crawl and reconcile broken references.

  C) Conventional Graph Databases (Neo4j, TigerGraph):
     Graph databases rely on physical pointer adjacency. An edge requires both
     source and destination vertices to exist in storage. Traversing a path
     where intermediate nodes have not arrived causes traversal aborts or null
     exceptions, rendering out-of-order distributed graph ingestion impractical.


3. CLEAVEDB'S CORE BREAKTHROUGH: THE ASYNCHRONOUS REFERENTIAL FABRIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The word "CLEAVE" is an auto-antonym (a Janus word):
  - Cleave (verb 1): To sever, divide, fracture, split apart.
  - Cleave (verb 2): To cling, adhere strongly, fuse, bind together.

CleaveDB uses this exact linguistic duality as its core architectural thesis:
IT IS AN ASYNCHRONOUS STRUCTURAL DATABASE WHERE RELATIONSHIPS ARE FIRST-CLASS,
DECOUPLED, AUTONOMOUS STATE MACHINES.

In CleaveDB:
  - A relationship is neither a static column constraint (SQL) nor a physical
    memory pointer (Graph).
  - A relationship is a "BOND": a stateful topological invariant that can exist
    in a state of *unresolved tension* (a Shadow Latch) before its target arrives,
    and then *snap into place* (Cleave-Snap) automatically, reactively, and
    atomically the moment the target lands.


4. FORMAL MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION: THE CLEAVE CALCULUS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 The Bond Semilattice
Let a Bond $b \in \mathcal{B}$ be a 6-tuple:
    $b = \langle s, d, \tau, \lambda, \epsilon, \sigma \rangle$
where:
  - $s \in \mathcal{V}$: Source Document Global Identifier (GID)
  - $d \in \mathcal{V}$: Destination Document GID
  - $\tau \in \mathcal{T}$: Bond Type / Relationship Label (e.g., "placed_by")
  - $\lambda \in \Lambda$: Lifetime / Lease Window ($\mathbb{R}^+ \cup \{\infty\}$)
  - $\epsilon \in \{\text{Soft}, \text{Firm}, \text{Strict}\}$: Enforcement Policy
  - $\sigma \in \Sigma$: Bond State

The state space $\Sigma$ forms a bounded join-semilattice $\langle \Sigma, \sqsubseteq, \sqcup \rangle$:
    $\Sigma = \{\bot, \text{Phantom}, \text{Latched}, \text{Fused}, \text{Severed}, \top\}$

Partial Ordering:
    $\bot \sqsubseteq \text{Phantom} \sqsubseteq \text{Latched} \sqsubseteq \text{Fused}$
    $\text{Latched} \sqsubseteq \text{Severed}$

Join Operator ($\sqcup$):
    For any two state observations $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$:
    - $\sigma_1 \sqcup \sigma_1 = \sigma_1$
    - $\text{Phantom} \sqcup \text{Latched} = \text{Latched}$
    - $\text{Latched} \sqcup \text{Fused} = \text{Fused}$
    - $\text{Fused} \sqcup \text{Severed} = \text{Severed}$ (Tombstone dominance)

4.2 Monotonic Convergence Theorem
Theorem 1 (Topological Closure Monotonicity):
Let $W = (w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n)$ be an arbitrary permutation of writes comprising
a set of documents $\mathcal{D}$ and bonds $\mathcal{B}$.
Because the bond transition function $\delta: \Sigma \times \mathcal{E} \to \Sigma$
is monotonic over the join-semilattice $\langle \Sigma, \sqsubseteq \rangle$,
the final topological graph state $G(\mathcal{D}, \mathcal{B})$ is deterministic
and independent of write ingestion order:
    $\forall \pi_1, \pi_2 \in \operatorname{Perm}(W): \operatorname{Fold}(\delta, \bot, \pi_1) = \operatorname{Fold}(\delta, \bot, \pi_2)$

Proof Sketch:
All bond transitions commute over the join operation. If document $d$ arrives
before source $s$, $d$ registers in the catalog; when $s$ arrives, $b$ transitions
directly from $\bot \to \text{Fused}$. If $s$ arrives before $d$, $b$ transitions
from $\bot \to \text{Phantom} \to \text{Latched}$; upon $d$'s arrival, $b$ joins with
the existence token of $d$ to transition $\text{Latched} \sqcup \text{Materialized}(d) \to \text{Fused}$.
By lattice monotonicity, both paths terminate at $\text{Fused}$.


5. STORAGE ENGINE INTERNALS & DATA STRUCTURE DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CleaveDB implements its storage engine in custom Rust (no SQLite, no RocksDB,
no external dependencies). It consists of four cooperative components:

5.1 The 16KB Page Architecture & Layout
Every storage file is partitioned into 16,384-byte slotted pages:
  - Header (64 bytes):
    * `page_id` (u64): Monotonic Page Identifier
    * `lsn` (u64): Log Sequence Number of the last modifying transaction
    * `page_type` (u8): Data (0x01), Index (0x02), PhantomLattice (0x03), Overflow (0x04)
    * `slot_count` (u16): Number of item entries in the page
    * `free_start` (u16): Pointer to start of free unallocated space
    * `free_end` (u16): Pointer to end of slotted records (growing bottom-up)
    * `crc32c` (u32): Castagnoli polynomial checksum covering the entire page

5.2 The Phantom Lattice B+Tree
The engine maintains two distinct structural indices:
  1. Materialized Bond Index:
     - `eout`: Key = `(src_gid: u64, rel_id: u32, dst_gid: u64)` -> Payload
     - `ein`:  Key = `(dst_gid: u64, rel_id: u32, src_gid: u64)` -> Payload
  2. The Phantom Lattice (Shadow Index):
     - Key = `(target_gid: u64, rel_id: u32, src_gid: u64)`
     - Value = `ShadowRecord { enforcement_tier, created_at_ts, lease_expiry_ts, flags }`

The Phantom Lattice is indexed by `target_gid`. When a query checks for the
target document, or when the target document is written, the storage engine
can probe the Phantom Lattice in $O(\log N)$ or $O(1)$ amortized page lookups.

5.3 The Latch Bloom Filter Cache
To prevent disk B+Tree lookups on every single write:
  - Each storage shard maintains an in-memory, highly compressed counting Bloom filter
    representing all currently unmaterialized target GIDs.
  - On write of any document $D$:
    * If `BloomFilter.contains(D.gid) == false`: Document is written without
      probing the Phantom Lattice. Overhead = 0 disk I/O.
    * If `BloomFilter.contains(D.gid) == true`: The engine performs a local
      probe into the Phantom Lattice, retrieves all pending shadow bonds, and
      executes the "Cleave-Snap" protocol.

5.4 The "Cleave-Snap" WAL Replay Protocol
When the target document arrives:
  1. The engine writes a `WAL_RECORD_POUR` for the target document.
  2. In the same group-commit batch, the engine emits `WAL_RECORD_BOND_RESOLVE`
     for each pending shadow bond.
  3. The shadow bond entry is removed from the Phantom Lattice, and the corresponding
     bidirectional entries are inserted into `eout` and `ein`.
  4. This entire transition is atomic to the local shard, requiring ZERO
     cross-network 2PC locks.


6. CLEAVEQL QUERY ALGEBRA & VOLCANO EXECUTION ENGINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Extended CleaveQL Grammar
CleaveQL is augmented with native operators for decoupled structural integrity:

  -- 1. Pouring with an explicit late-binding bond
  POUR INTO orders "ord_992" {
    "total": 450.00,
    "sku": "MACBOOK-M3"
  } BONDING TO users "usr_42" AS "purchased_by" ENFORCEMENT FIRM LEASE 24 HOURS;

  -- 2. Querying across mixed topologies (resolved + pending)
  SCOOP orders WHERE total > 100
  TRAVERSING "purchased_by"
  ALLOWING UNRESOLVED;

  -- 3. Inspecting the Phantom Lattice (Structural Telemetry)
  SHOW PENDING BONDS FOR users;

  -- 4. Reconciling or forcing bond state
  HEAL BONDS ON orders TIMEOUT EXPIRED;

6.2 The Volcano Iterator Engine with Late-Binding Nodes
The CleaveDB Volcano query planner introduces three novel execution nodes:

  1. `LateBindingJoinNode`:
     Evaluates joins where the right-hand child may contain unmaterialized
     shadow vertices. If `ALLOWING UNRESOLVED` is specified, it produces a
     virtualized tuple: `Tuple(order_data, VirtualNode { status: "pending", gid: "usr_42" })`.
     If strict mode is requested, it applies early-pruning to skip pending paths.

  2. `ShadowScanNode`:
     Scans the Phantom Lattice directly for administrative auditing, SLA monitoring,
     and compensating reconciliation jobs.

  3. `SpeculativeFilterNode`:
     Evaluates predicates against speculative shadow states, allowing applications
     to preview the state of the graph *as if all pending bonds had fused*.


7. BOND LIFECYCLE, INVARIANT ENFORCEMENT TIERS & FAILURE MODES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not all relationships in an enterprise have the same consistency requirements.
CleaveDB formalizes three distinct operational tiers:

7.1 Soft Bonds (Observational / Fluid)
  - Purpose: Non-critical associations (e.g., social graph likes, recommendations, tags).
  - Write behavior: Never fails; zero write stalls.
  - Read behavior: Returns `None` or virtual placeholder if target does not exist.
  - Lifecycle: Remains in Phantom Lattice indefinitely until target lands or source is drained.

7.2 Firm Bonds (SLA-Leased / Eventual Consistency)
  - Purpose: Business workflows (e.g., payment references, order-to-customer links).
  - Write behavior: Writes immediately; attaches a lease duration $\lambda$ (e.g., 2 hours).
  - Lifecycle:
    * If target lands before $\lambda$ expires: Fuses normally into active graph.
    * If target FAILS to arrive before $\lambda$ expires: The bond transitions to
      `Expired` state. CleaveDB emits a system webhook event or moves the source
      document into a designated `_quarantine` bucket for compensation.

7.3 Strict Bonds (Speculative Quarantine)
  - Purpose: Mission-critical ledger entries (e.g., balance transfers, inventory dispatch).
  - Write behavior: Document is ingested, but its visibility is restricted.
  - Lifecycle: The document is stored in a private transaction shadow layer.
    External queries do not observe the document until its strict bonds achieve
    referential closure. Once the target arrives, the entire cluster promotes
    the document atomically.


8. GARBAGE COLLECTION, CYCLE DETECTION & ORPHAN MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In asynchronous distributed graphs, two structural pathology risks emerge:
  1. Permanent Phantom Orphans (a target is promised but never created).
  2. Circular Phantom Dependencies (Doc A bonds to Doc B; Doc B bonds to Doc A;
     neither exists yet).

CleaveDB solves both pathologies natively:

8.1 Epoch-Based Phantom Sweeping
CleaveDB groups all shadow latches into generational time epochs (e.g., 1-hour
buckets). When an epoch's lease expires:
  - The engine's background compaction worker scans only the active expired epoch.
  - Unclaimed firm bonds are processed according to bucket policy:
    * Mode A (Sever): The shadow latch is dropped, and the edge is severed.
    * Mode B (Cascade Drain): The source document is moved to `_rubbish`.
    * Mode C (Compensate): A dead-letter queue (DLQ) event is pushed via WebSockets.

8.2 Phantom Cycle Resolution
Because primary identifiers in CleaveDB are 63-bit BLAKE2b hashes (`gid_of(coll/id)`),
graph cycles can be detected even before documents exist.
The storage engine runs an asynchronous cycle-detection scanner using a modified
Tarjan's strongly connected components (SCC) algorithm over the combined graph:
    $G_{\text{unified}} = G_{\text{materialized}} \cup G_{\text{phantom}}$
Cycles among purely phantom nodes are flagged and surfaced via `SHOW PHANTOM CYCLES`.


9. TARGET DOMAIN & INDUSTRIAL ARCHITECTURE CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Where does CleaveDB fundamentally outperform every other database?

9.1 Case Study 1: E-Commerce Microservice Sagas
  - The Problem Today:
    The Checkout Service emits an `OrderPlaced` event. The Payment Service emits
    a `PaymentCaptured` event. The Shipping Service emits a `LabelCreated` event.
    Under high load, network partitions cause `PaymentCaptured` or `LabelCreated`
    to reach the database *before* `OrderPlaced`.
    In PostgreSQL, the foreign key fails; in MongoDB, documents sit unlinked and
    require expensive batch aggregation pipelines to stitch together.
  - The CleaveDB Solution:
    Each service independently writes its document and creates a Firm Bond to
    the Order ID. Whichever event arrives first creates the base; subsequent
    events snap into place via Cleave-Snap. Referential integrity is guaranteed
    at wire speed without distributed transactions or Outbox patterns.

9.2 Case Study 2: Distributed Edge & IoT Fleet Telemetry
  - The Problem Today:
    Sensors on oil rigs, airplanes, or delivery vehicles record telemetric events
    referencing central asset IDs and inspection batches while disconnected from the cloud.
    When 100,000 edge devices reconnect simultaneously, they blast millions of
    interdependent records out of chronological sequence.
  - The CleaveDB Solution:
    CleaveDB's Phantom Lattice ingests the telemetry burst without waiting for
    central catalog synchronization. As master asset catalogs update, the entire
    fleet graph converges monotonically to a consistent state.

9.3 Case Study 3: Complex Supply-Chain Provenance (Cleaving & Fusing)
  - The Problem Today:
    A chemical batch or semiconductor wafer lot is split ("cleaved") into dozens
    of sub-lots, blended with other components ("fused"), and distributed across
    global supply chains. Tracking multi-hop recall provenance across 50 splits
    and merges crashes relational CTEs and graph databases alike.
  - The CleaveDB Solution:
    CleaveDB tracks both directional Bonds and topological Cleavages as native
    storage primitives, enabling $O(1)$ amortized ancestry and recall traversals.
